Frequently Asked Airline Power Bank Questions

Q. Is a 20,000 mAh power bank allowed on airplanes?

Yes! At standard 3.7V nominal Li-ion voltage, a 20,000 mAh power bank equals 74.0 Wh (20,000 × 3.7 ÷ 1,000 = 74 Wh), which sits safely under the universal 100 Wh carry-on limit on TSA/FAA, EASA, Korean Air, Delta, Emirates, and ANA. Always carry it in cabin baggage—never checked luggage.

Q. What is the maximum mAh power bank allowed on a plane without airline approval?

Airlines regulate batteries by Watt-hours (Wh), capping unapproved carry-on batteries at 100 Wh. At 3.7V, 100 Wh equals 27,027 mAh—which is why travel laptop banks are sized at 26,800 mAh (99.16 Wh), 27,000 mAh (99.9 Wh), or 27,650 mAh at 3.6V (99.54 Wh). A 30,000 mAh bank at 3.7V is 111 Wh and requires check-in counter approval (max 2 units).

Q. What are South Korea's 2025–2026 power bank zip-lock & overhead bin rules?

On all Korean airlines (Korean Air, Asiana, Jeju Air, Jin Air, T'way, Air Premia, Air Busan), passengers must NEVER store power banks in overhead bins, must insulate ports using clear zip-lock pouches or tape, cannot charge power banks in-flight, and may bring up to 5 units ≤100 Wh without approval (or max 2 units of 100.1–160 Wh with check-in inspection stickers).

Q. Why do 10,000 mAh power banks only charge a 5,000 mAh phone ~1.6 times instead of twice?

The mAh rating on a power bank is measured at its internal 3.7V cell voltage (37 Wh). Boosting 3.7V up to 5V/9V/15V USB-C PD and regulating voltage inside your phone's battery charger consumes roughly 15–20% energy as heat—leaving ~82% net usable Wh (30.3 Wh), or ~1.56 full charges for a 19.4 Wh flagship phone.
