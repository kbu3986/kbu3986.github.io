About WattCheck, Data Methodology & Contact

WattCheck was built to eliminate airport security confiscations caused by confusing mAh vs. Wh battery labels and misleading power bank capacity claims.

1. Engineering Formulas & 82% Efficiency Model

Simply dividing a 10,000 mAh power bank by a 5,000 mAh phone battery overestimates real-world charges by ~20–30%. WattCheck computes true energy transfer in Watt-hours (Wh):

1) Rated Wh = (mAh × Nominal Cell Voltage V) ÷ 1,000
2) Net Usable Wh = Rated Wh × (DC-DC Conversion Efficiency % ÷ 100)
3) Full 0–100% Charges = Net Usable Wh ÷ Target Device Battery Wh

Our 82% default efficiency reflects lab-tested USB-C PD boost conversion plus cable and phone PMIC charging losses, with an interactive 65%–95% slider for custom testing.

2. Primary Aviation Regulatory Sources

  • IATA Dangerous Goods Regulations (DGR 2.3.5.9 / Table 2.3.A): Global 100 Wh carry-on threshold & 100.1–160 Wh airline approval band (max 2 units).
  • South Korea MOLIT (국토교통부 2025–2026 항공 안전 표준안): Overhead bin storage ban, terminal tape / clear zip-lock pouch mandate, and 5-piece ≤100 Wh cap across Korean carriers.
  • US FAA PackSafe / TSA, EASA, Japan MLIT & China CAAC (3C Rule): Country-specific enforcement updates including Japan's July 2025 bin ban, Singapore/Emirates in-flight bans, and China's June 2025 CCC mark mandate.
